Grades tell us how students did in one class. Program assessment tells us how the program is doing: across every section, how well do students' final ePortfolios show the outcomes we all teach toward?

How we sample

Each term we draw a random sample of ePortfolios from every section. We size the sample to keep results within a 5-point margin of error at 75% confidence or higher, and we flag any term that falls short.

How we score

Raters calibrate together, then score each ePortfolio on every student learning outcome and give it a holistic score. A score of 4 to 6 on our six-point scale (agree or higher) means the portfolio demonstrates the outcome.

Closing the loop

Assessment matters when it changes what we do. Here is how these results have shaped the program so far.

  1. 2023 to 2025

    The first two years of results pointed to the same outcomes: Writing and Power in ENC 1101, Revision in both courses, and Contributing Knowledge in ENC 1102.

  2. 2025 to 2026

    The program named those outcomes as priorities and focused professional development and curriculum on them.

  3. 2026 to 2027

    With three years of data showing Writing and Power and Revision still trailing, faculty are piloting revised outcomes. Writing and Power becomes Critical Language Awareness, and revision is built into Writing Processes (ENC 1101) and Reflection & Writing Transfer (ENC 1102).

  4. Summer 2027

    We will assess the pilot with ePortfolios and compare the results with this baseline.

Results guide decisions about curriculum, assignments, the ePortfolio, professional development, and norming. They never evaluate individual teachers, sections, or students.

    Calibration and rater agreement

    Each term's scoring happens in two stages. First, calibration: 35 portfolios from the random sample are read by two raters, and when the two scores on an outcome are not adjacent (two or more points apart), a third reader scores it and that score becomes final. Once calibration and adjudication are complete, the rest of the sample is distributed and each portfolio is read by one scorer. Agreement can only be measured on the calibration set, so these figures show how consistently raters were applying the rubric going into the single reads.

      About the data and methods

      What was assessed

      Final ePortfolios from ENC 1101 (Fall 2023, Fall 2024, Fall 2025) and ENC 1102 (Spring 2024, Spring 2025, Fall 2025, Spring 2026), drawn at random from each term's course roster. Each portfolio received a score on six student learning outcomes and a holistic score on a six-point scale: 6 very strongly agree, 5 strongly agree, 4 agree, 3 disagree, 2 strongly disagree, 1 very strongly disagree. There is no neutral midpoint.

      How portfolios were scored

      Scoring ran in two stages each term. In calibration, 35 sampled portfolios were each read by two raters; where the two scores on an outcome were not adjacent, a third reader's score became final, and otherwise the final score is the average of the two reads. After calibration, the remaining sampled portfolios were distributed and read once. From Fall 2024 on, results use the final scores recorded in Assigned Reads. For Fall 2023 and Spring 2024, whose workbooks keep the raw reads instead, final calibration scores were computed by the same rule. Either way, results include the 35 calibration portfolios, since they are part of the random sample. An averaged score of 3.5 counts as not demonstrating the outcome; these split decisions are counted in the tables. In the bar charts, averaged scores between two levels are shown at the lower level.

      Unscorable portfolios

      Drawn portfolios that were broken links, inaccessible, not submitted, or from students who withdrew stay in the data as unscored, read from the color codes and notes in the program's workbooks. Interrater portfolios replaced by an alternate are counted as drawn and unscored; the alternate is counted as scored.

      Groups

      Delivery mode comes from section codes, as the program codes them: four-digit codes (and honors sections) are face-to-face, codes with M are mixed mode, W fully online, and V video streaming. Groups with fewer than 10 scored portfolios are not reported. Results are never broken out by section or instructor.

      Statistical significance

      A statistically significant difference is one unlikely to be due to chance in which portfolios happened to be sampled. Each comparison is tested two ways: whether the overall pattern of ratings differs (rank-based tests suited to an ordinal scale) and whether the agree-or-higher rate differs. P-values are adjusted for multiple comparisons (Holm). A result that is not significant means no difference was detected, not that there is none; small groups may be too small to detect a real difference.

      Comparing terms

      Pooled course views combine terms and test differences between them. The outcomes, rating scale, and norming process were the same in every term, so term-to-term comparisons within a course are valid. Differences between terms can still reflect who enrolled, which sections were sampled, or which raters read, as well as changes in teaching.
